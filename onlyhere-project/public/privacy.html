2. Using Gemlyx without an account

You can build a guide, read it, print it and share it without ever registering. When you do that:

  • Nothing is stored on our servers under your name, because we do not have your name.
  • Your browser keeps things on your own device: your theme choice, anything you save, and any guide you have not attached to an account. This is your device's own storage, it never reaches us, and clearing your browser data removes it.
  • What you type into the chat is sent to the AI providers in section 9 so that a guide can be written from it.
  • Our hosting providers keep ordinary server logs. As with every website, requests to Gemlyx are logged by the companies that host it, and those logs contain IP addresses, which count as personal data. We do not copy them into our own database, do not link them to accounts, and do not use them to build any picture of a visitor. They exist so that the service can be operated and abuse can be stopped.

We set no cookies of our own, and there is no cookie banner because there is no cookie to consent to. The sign-in session of an account holder is kept in the browser's own local storage rather than in a cookie.

4. What Gemlyx notices about you

This section describes the one thing on this page that you did not type in yourself, so it is worth reading closely.

When a guide is successfully built, Gemlyx records what that trip was about: its themes and the ways of travelling it used. Nothing else. Four rules govern it. The first three are built into the code rather than promised here. The fourth is not built yet, which is explained below:

  • Only your own side of the conversation is ever used. Never anything Gemlyx said back to you.
  • One trip is not a preference. Something has to be observed at least twice before it counts for anything.
  • What you typed always beats what was noticed. The two are stored separately and described to the AI separately, and an observation never overwrites an answer you gave.
  • It has to be visible to you and reversible.

Observations that have settled are added to the instructions for your next guide, marked as observed rather than stated. They are stored on your account row and go when your account goes.

Being straight about the fourth rule. The screen that lets you see and clear these observations yourself is not built yet. Until it is, write to privacy@gemlyxtravel.com and we will send you what has been noticed, or delete it, within the time limit in section 15. You may also object to this processing entirely, in which case it stops for your account. An earlier version of this page said Gemlyx built no picture of you from your behaviour. That stopped being true when this feature started working, and this section is the correction.

5. Location

If your browser asks and you agree, Gemlyx reads your location to work out whether you are currently in Denmark and roughly how far you are from a town. It is used on the page you are looking at, is not sent to our database, and is not kept. Say no and everything else still works. You can withdraw the permission in your browser at any time.

6. Asking Gemlyx a question

Guides and place pages carry a box for asking a question about what is on the page. If you are signed in when you use it, the question, the place it was asked about and your account identifier are recorded. There is a daily limit on questions, and this is how it is counted. It is the only way to stop one person exhausting a service that costs real money per question. The record is deleted when your account is deleted.

7. Sharing a guide

A shared guide link is unlisted, not secret. Search engines are told not to index it, and the preview services behind WhatsApp, Messenger and the rest are allowed through so the link looks like something when you send it. Anyone who has the link can read the guide, so treat it like a door key rather than a password. If you filled in a name, it can appear in the text of the guide.

To write your guide

This is the part worth reading twice. To turn a conversation into a guide, the text of that conversation and your profile summary, if you filled one in, are sent to:

  • Anthropic, which writes the guide text
  • OpenAI, which plans the structure and flags weak writing
  • Perplexity and Tavily, which research and fact-check what goes into it

They are sent only what is needed to produce the guide or the answer. They are not sent your email address or your account identity. Please do not type anything into the chat that you would not want processed by those companies.

To get the facts right

  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim for turning a place name into a coordinate
  • Weather providers for the forecast on your dates
  • Wikimedia Commons for photographs
  • Ticketmaster for whether an event is on sale
  • Firecrawl, which reads public web pages we could not read directly

If this list changes, this page changes on the same day and the date at the top moves with it.

10. Transfers outside the EU

Data is stored inside the EU where the provider offers it. Several of the companies in section 9 are established in the United States, so using Gemlyx means some personal data is processed outside the EU.

Those transfers rely on the safeguards in Chapter V of the GDPR: the European Commission's adequacy decision for the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified under it, and otherwise the Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, which form part of the data processing terms we have agreed with each provider. If that matters to you, you can use Gemlyx entirely without an account, and nothing identifying you is sent anywhere.

12. What we do not do

  • No advertising. No ad network, no display ads, no sponsored placements. Nothing is sold or shared with data brokers. Booking links that pay a commission are a different thing and are explained in section 13.
  • No analytics and no tracking pixels. There is no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, no session recorder.
  • No cookies of our own, and therefore no cookie banner. Google may set its own cookies during a Google sign-in, on Google's pages.
  • No tracking you across other websites, and no buying of data about you from anyone else.
  • No marketing email. If the paid tier described in the Terms starts, we will email you about the trip you asked us to watch, and nothing else.
  • No selling, and no training. Your chat text and your profile are never sold, and are never used to train or fine-tune an AI model.

What Gemlyx does notice about you is described in section 4, and it is deliberately the only thing in this area that is not a flat no.

13. Affiliate links

Some links carry a partner code. If you book through one, Gemlyx may earn a commission. It costs you nothing and the price is the same as it would be without the code. Today that is Tiqets, for admission to museums, castles and gardens, and it is the only one. The Booking.com and Ticketmaster links in the product carry no partner code at the moment and earn Gemlyx nothing, and the Airbnb link is a plain search link that never has. If that changes, this page changes on the same day.

There are two things this does not do, and they are the ones worth stating plainly:

  • It does not decide what Gemlyx recommends. Which places are written about, which are rated "Can't miss" and which are marked "only if you're nearby" are editorial decisions, and are made before any link exists. Nothing ranks higher because it pays, and nothing is left out because it does not. Plenty of what Gemlyx recommends earns nothing at all.
  • It does not track you. The code is part of the web address you click. Gemlyx sets no cookie to follow you and does not learn from these links who you are or what you booked. Once you are on the partner's site, you are covered by that company's privacy policy, not this one.

Every paid link says so where it sits, in one line, rather than only here. Where a link earns nothing, it says that too.

14. Artificial intelligence

The chat, the question box and the guides themselves are run by AI systems, not by a person. Nothing you send reaches a human at Gemlyx unless you email us.

Automated processing decides what goes into your guide. No automated decision is taken about you that produces a legal effect or anything similarly significant, so the special rules in Article 22 of the GDPR do not apply. If you would rather a person looked at something, write to us.

15. Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask us to give you a copy of what we hold about you, correct it, delete it, hand it over in a portable form, or restrict what we do with it. You can also object to processing based on legitimate interests, including everything in section 4. Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time, and withdrawing it does not affect what was done beforehand.

Write to privacy@gemlyxtravel.com. We answer within one month, and will tell you if a request needs longer than that. There is no charge.

If you are unhappy with the answer, you can complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency: Datatilsynet, Carl Jacobsens Vej 35, 2500 Valby, datatilsynet.dk. You can also complain to the authority in the EU country where you live.

16. Children

Gemlyx is not aimed at children, and an account may not be created by anyone under 15. Danish law sets the floor at 13 for a service like this one; 15 is our own stricter line. If you believe a child has an account, write to us and it will be removed.

17. Changes to this policy

A privacy promise that quietly goes stale is worse than one never made. When what Gemlyx collects changes, this page changes with it, and the version and date at the top are how you check.

Version 1.0 said that the profile was offered after the account existed and was entirely optional, that an age band was stored rather than a date of birth, and that Gemlyx built no picture of anyone from their behaviour. Registration now asks for a name, a date of birth and a gender, and the feature described in section 4 went live. All three statements are corrected above rather than quietly removed.
